1. How to read a task card

A card reports the literal compiled/default B=1 and the frozen B=5 selection for SAP Warp and MuJoCo Warp. “Accuracy” is shown only for tasks with preregistered discrete outcomes; bounce and toss instead report their continuous fidelity measure. The final subsection is not a tuning footnote: it states what the selected setting did, or did not, transfer to.

Reporting boundary. B=5 is bounded task usability under a declared candidate budget. It is not a universal simulator ranking. Force-, friction-, and penetration-based claims remain pending until their required telemetry audit is complete.

3. Static friction

These tasks test whether contact models preserve or release a body at the expected frictional threshold. Primary evidence is discrete retain/release agreement plus a mechanism metric such as creep or preload.

3.1 Three-box push

1. Task setup

Three identical boxes start in a centred vertical stack. A horizontal force acts at the middle or top box COM after 0.6 s of settling, ramps for 0.4 s and holds for 0.65 s. Baseline: 1 kg per box, 20-mm half-width, dt=0.5 ms. Labels include retain, slip and tip; analytic slip and tipping thresholds define cases with a margin from the competing event. Classification uses the first event: contact-face displacement ≥1 mm or box tilt >10°. A non-finite state is invalid and never counts as correct.

Parameter or metricDefinition
Physical sweepbox mass 0.5/1/2 kg; friction μ=0.3/0.5/0.7/0.9; box half-width 15/20/30 mm; middle/top loads; dt=0.25/0.5/1 ms. These are the declared 25 combinations, not a full Cartesian grid.
Valid and correctfinite state, no capacity overflow, pre-event pushed-interface contact duty ≥99%, and class matches the analytic label
Creepslope of contact-face displacement over the final 0.4-s hold; the retain selection cell is reported separately from sliding cases

3.2 Simple pinch

1. Task setup

Two opposed pads close on a block, establish preload, and lift it 60 mm. The mechanism is whether normal preload creates enough friction to retain the block. The reported frozen-setting scopes are not the same grid: SAP fixes compression at 0.2 mm and sweeps five masses (0.15, 0.20, 0.25, 0.30, 0.40 kg), R=20 each; MuJoCo Warp uses a separate 17-condition outcome-label grid that varies preload, mass, friction, and timestep, also R=20 each.

4. Sustained contact

This category probes long-lived support contacts: numerical overlap, load closure, settling, and whether a calibration setting survives thickness and timestep changes.

5. Dynamic impact

Impact tasks report event and trajectory fidelity rather than forcing a binary accuracy: valid rebound/apex recovery for bounce, and held-out trajectory error for toss.

5.1 GAUGE ball bounce

1. Task setup

A released GAUGE object impacts a board. A valid rebound apex is a local maximum of the ball-centre height only after first contact has ended, the ball has separated into free flight, and its height is above board + radius. For each trajectory, pair the first up-to-three simulated and reference apexes and compute Eapex = mean(|hsim - href| / max(|href|, 1e-6 m)). This is continuous relative apex-height/energy-decay error, not a classification: no preregistered threshold defines a correct/incorrect bounce. If either trace has no valid apex, Eapex is undefined (not zero).

6. Composite insertion

Insertion couples clearance, contact geometry, friction, compliance, and actuation. A rollout is credible only when the peg enters without excessive peak/final penetration, radial escape, or contact-capacity truncation.

6.1 Round socket (peg-in-hole)

1. Task setup

An 80-g cylindrical peg, initially tilted 1°, drops for 2 s into a shared 32-piece CoACD round socket. Selection is 2-mm radial clearance with 1-mm lateral offset; R=20 perturbations. Credibility requires entry, radial error within clearance, no overflow, and both peak and final penetration at most 10% of clearance.

Credible selection insertion. A rollout counts as credible only if all gates pass: it reaches the insertion-depth / coarse-entry gate (z ≤ 45 mm, radial error ≤ 3 mm), final radial error is within its clearance r ≤ c, it does not escape, there is no contact-capacity overflow/truncation, and both peak and final penetration are ≤ 0.1c. Thus 19/20 means 19 of the 20 independent lateral-perturbation rollouts pass every gate; it is a success count, not a clearance value.
